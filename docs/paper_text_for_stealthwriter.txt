PART 1: ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding certified electricians, plumbers, and mechanics in secondary Indian cities remains an everyday struggle. In municipal hubs like Rajahmundry, most households still hire tradesmen through roadside contacts or informal neighborhood leads. This reliance on unverified channels leaves homeowners exposed to unpredictable pricing, questionable craftsmanship, and long arrival delays, while skilled artisans face erratic daily earnings. Mainstream on-demand platforms rarely serve these tier-2 and tier-3 urban markets well; their models favor dense metropolitan centers, static catalog booking, and high commission cuts that alienate local technicians. To address these municipal market frictions, we present Workify, a decoupled, cloud-native dispatch and reputation framework engineered specifically for localized municipal jurisdictions. The architecture combines three core mechanisms: (1) a bounded Haversine geospatial engine that restricts technician discovery to a strict 10 km municipal boundary across defined locality zones; (2) a bilateral Elo rating formulation that adjusts both worker effort scores (SES) and customer behavior scores (CBS) after every job, curbing the runaway score inflation typical of standard 5-star reviews; and (3) a multi-criteria ranking function balancing travel proximity, verified vocational trade certifications (such as ITI diplomas), on-duty availability, and historical Elo standing. To protect domestic privacy and enforce physical accountability, Workify requires a cryptographic one-time password (OTP) check-in at the customer's doorstep before work can begin or escrow funds can disburse. Evaluation across 20 municipal localities in Rajahmundry shows an average matchmaking latency of 34.8 ms---an 81.1% reduction compared to conventional spatial KNN approaches---with 96.4% radial precision and complete prevention of proxy worker substitution.

PART 2: SECTION 1 - INTRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Secondary and tertiary urban centers across India are undergoing rapid residential expansion. In municipal cities such as Rajahmundry, Kakinada, and Vijayawada in Andhra Pradesh, homeowners require regular domestic trade support---from fixing leaking pipelines and rewiring short circuits to servicing split-unit air conditioners. Yet while algorithm-driven applications have thoroughly reshaped municipal cab booking and restaurant deliveries, the local blue-collar service sector remains informal and fragmented. Daily hiring still runs through roadside worker corners, local hardware shop referrals, or frayed word-of-mouth networks [1]. For residential households, this reliance produces constant anxiety over technician competence, erratic pricing, and no-show workers.

Hiring a tradesman differs sharply from hailing a taxi. A cab ride lasts fifteen to thirty minutes in an automobile governed by regional motor vehicle standards. In contrast, home maintenance requires artisans to bring specialized tools, diagnose concealed faults behind walls or under floors, and remain inside a private dwelling for hours. These practical realities create four chronic friction points:

1. Unverified Skills and Opaque Backgrounds: Homeowners have virtually no reliable way to verify whether a visiting electrician or plumber has undergone formal vocational training, such as an Industrial Training Institute (ITI) trade course or a recognized National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) certification.
2. Inconsistent and Disputed Pricing: Without clear upfront rate schedules, repair bills often swing widely. Customers frequently encounter disputed travel add-ons, sudden material markups, or steep premiums during urgent evening repairs.
3. Mobilization Delays and Phantom Availability: Printed directories or static classified apps list phone numbers but cannot track whether a tradesman is on duty, already occupied with an all-day job across town, or available to respond promptly.
4. Doorstep Safety and Worker Accountability: Because informal bookings involve no verified record of dispatch, residents worry about personal security, while technicians have little protection against unpaid bills or unfounded damage claims. In some cases, unvetted apprentices show up in place of the experienced tradesman who took the initial call.

To bridge these gaps, we designed Workify ("Skilled Workers. Better Tomorrow."), an integrated cloud-native system targeted specifically at municipal trade networks.

1.1 Key Technical Contributions:
- Constrained Haversine Search: We develop a bounding-box pre-filtered Haversine routine that evaluates trade candidates within a strict 10 km municipal boundary, achieving dispatch filtering in under 50 ms across 20 calibrated municipal zones in Rajahmundry.
- Bilateral Elo Reputation Model: Moving away from inflated 5-star averages, we introduce a two-sided Elo rating system that updates technician performance (SES) and customer conduct (CBS) simultaneously, creating a stable, cheat-resistant quality metric.
- Multi-Criteria Dispatch Ranking: We formulate a multi-factor ranking function that weights transit proximity, historical Elo standing, customer reviews, verified trade qualifications (ITI/Govt certificates), years of experience, and live duty status.
- Doorstep Authentication Protocol: We implement a time-sensitive, 4-digit arrival token (OTP) generated on the client device. Entering this code on-site verifies physical presence, triggers an atomic transition from PENDING to IN_PROGRESS, and protects payments via escrow.
- Production Cloud Architecture: We build a modular three-tier software stack pairing a React 18 / OpenStreetMap Leaflet frontend with Django REST APIs, a dedicated FastAPI vector engine, and Amazon S3 dual-bucket storage for public assets and encrypted KYC documents.

PART 3: SECTION 2 - RELATED WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Spatial Crowdsourcing and Hyperlocal Dispatch:
Spatial crowdsourcing coordinates mobile workers to complete physical duties at specific geographical coordinates [1], [19]-[21]. While researchers have explored task assignment extensively for ride-hailing and courier fleets [2], home repair dispatch presents an entirely different set of operational constraints. Artisans do not pick up and drop off passengers along continuous corridors; they transport bulky tools and replacement parts to a single residential location, staying on-site for 45 to 180 minutes per visit. Consequently, tight municipal neighborhood clustering matters far more than dynamic en-route rerouting. Prominent platforms such as Urban Company and TaskRabbit operate primarily in large metropolitan centers [3]. Their centralized allocation structures frequently levy steep commissions (often 20-30%) while reducing skilled, independent tradesmen to interchangeable gig workers.

2.2 Recommender Systems and The Rating Inflation Dilemma:
Collaborative Filtering (CF) and Matrix Factorization (SVD) form the bedrock of algorithmic recommendations [4]. However, these algorithms struggle in municipal trade markets due to extreme interaction sparsity. Because a typical home hires a plumber or electrician only two or three times a year, customer-worker interaction matrices are overwhelmingly sparse, causing standard collaborative filters to suffer severe cold-start issues. In parallel, conventional 5-star rating mechanisms inevitably succumb to social-influence rating inflation [5]. When the overwhelming majority of reviews drift to 4.8 or 5.0 stars, ratings lose their ability to distinguish experienced master artisans from novices. To overcome this distortion, Workify incorporates principles from competitive Elo ratings [6] and modern crowdsourcing reputation frameworks [22], [23], translating completed bookings into dynamic, zero-sum reputation adjustments based on job complexity and mutual conduct.

2.3 Physical Security and Doorstep Trust Verification:
Personal safety during home service visits is a well-recognized barrier to gig platform adoption [15]. Most commercial services rely solely on automated SMS alerts or phone numbers, providing no tamper-proof guarantee that the arriving technician is actually the individual shown in the profile. Workify addresses this risk with an atomic, two-factor arrival handshake built on HMAC-SHA256 tokens, accompanied by Amazon S3 pre-signed storage for verified identity and trade documents. This workflow ensures that unvetted substitute workers cannot initiate billing or access private premises under someone else's name.

2.4 Gap Identification:
Prior literature has investigated spatial k-nearest neighbor (KNN) indexes [7] and theoretical routing models for urban fleets [2]. However, existing work rarely tackles the combined problem of localized multi-factor trade ranking, two-sided reputation balancing, and physical proof-of-dispatch within medium-sized municipal centers. Workify addresses this operational void directly.

PART 4: SECTION 3 - METHODOLOGY (DESCRIPTIVE NARRATIVE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Municipal Geographic Scope and Problem Setting:
We set our operational scope across the municipal territory of Rajahmundry, a prominent urban district in Andhra Pradesh, India. The municipal area is divided into 20 verified local wards and neighborhoods (such as Danavaipeta, Morampudi, Innespeta, Aryapuram, Prakash Nagar, and Dowleswaram). Within this territory, the platform maintains a pool of technicians across seven core domestic disciplines: plumbing, electrical systems, carpentry, masonry, air conditioning/refrigeration (HVAC), appliance repair, and home sanitation.

3.3 Bilateral Elo Dynamic Rating Concept:
Both workers and customers maintain continuous ratings starting from an initial baseline of 1200. Before each service engagement, expected outcome probabilities are calculated using the logistic sigmoid. Upon task completion, the Servicer Effort Score (SES) and the Customer Behaviour Score (CBS) update their respective ratings simultaneously using a sensitivity parameter of 32. This formulation ensures that a technician who delivers high craftsmanship under difficult conditions earns substantial rating gains, while uncooperative customer conduct or sloppy repairs result in downward adjustments.

3.5 Zero-Trust Physical Verification Protocol:
As soon as a client confirms a booking, the system derives a time-sensitive, 4-digit arrival passcode using HMAC-SHA256 based on a secret key, booking ID, and timestamp. When the technician arrives at the home, entering this passcode triggers an atomic database state transition from PENDING to IN_PROGRESS. This validates physical presence on-site, starts the billable work timer, and activates escrow safeguards.

3.6 Three-Tier Cloud-Native Architecture:
Workify implements a modular three-tier software architecture adhering to modern distributed systems design principles:
- Presentation Tier: A responsive single-page web client built with React 18 and Vite. It incorporates an interactive OpenStreetMap Leaflet map featuring custom animated markers (DivIcon) that allow users to inspect technician locations and filter by locality ward.
- Application Tier: A Django 5.0 REST backend managing user authentication and core business rules, coupled with an asynchronous FastAPI microservice optimized for vector calculations and real-time Elo updates.
- Data Tier: A PostgreSQL 15 relational database storing application entities, a Redis 7.0 in-memory cache for live worker presence, and Amazon S3 dual-bucket storage. Public UI media is stored separately from private KYC records (such as Aadhaar and ITI diplomas), which are guarded by 15-minute expiring pre-signed URLs.

PART 5: SECTION 4 - RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Detection and Matchmaking Latency Performance:
We benchmarked Workify under simulated municipal workloads comprising 100 verified tradesmen and 500 concurrent dispatch requests distributed across 20 municipal zones in Rajahmundry. Workify registers an average dispatch execution latency of 34.8 ms. This represents an 81.1% reduction in latency compared to spatial KNN search (184.2 ms) and an 85.5% reduction compared to collaborative filtering (240.6 ms). Furthermore, because the pre-filtered Haversine routine prunes out-of-boundary candidates prior to composite scoring, radial precision reaches 96.4%, ensuring that matched technicians can realistically reach the customer without crossing unpredictable transit barriers.

4.2 Explanation Fidelity and Rating Stability:
Tradesmen who maintained steady service quality experienced a consistent score progression from 1200 to 1840 Elo points. Rather than clustering indistinguishably near a 5.0 ceiling, the scores formed clear performance tiers, offering users an intuitive and tamper-resistant quality signal.

4.3 Cross-Method Agreement and Security Validation:
Because a booking cannot transition to IN_PROGRESS without the customer-held OTP, the platform achieved 100% elimination of proxy worker substitutions. In addition, arrival dispute incidents dropped by 76.4%, while automated escrow payouts settled in an average of 1.18 seconds once both parties confirmed task completion.

4.4 Explanation and Dispatch Efficiency:
The in-memory Redis cache handled 2,400 requests per second at 1.82 ms latency, facilitating instant worker availability lookups. In parallel, the FastAPI calculation microservice sustained 480 requests per second at 34.8 ms latency, confirming its capacity to manage peak booking traffic in municipal centers without forming request queues.

4.6 Qualitative Field Example:
During field testing in Rajahmundry, a resident in Danavaipeta reported an urgent kitchen plumbing leak. The platform processed the request and retrieved top candidate matches in 32 ms. The highest-ranked technician---Ramesh Das, who brings 12 years of trade experience, an ITI certificate, and an Elo rating of 1840---was stationed 1.8 km away in Innespeta. He accepted the dispatch and arrived at the home in 14 minutes. Once the resident shared the 4-digit doorstep passcode, the system verified his arrival, started the work timer, and committed the agreed fee to escrow. Following the repair, both parties exchanged ratings, updating their bilateral Elo scores in real time.

PART 6: SECTION 5 - LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
While our findings demonstrate clear operational benefits, several limitations should be noted. First, the 10 km operational radius fits compact urban centers like Rajahmundry well, but larger municipal areas may need adaptive radius dilation that accounts for live bridge traffic and seasonal road congestion. Second, tradesmen in regional markets often carry budget mobile devices; frontend components must maintain smooth performance and graceful offline degradation on unstable 2G and 3G connections. Finally, the bilateral Elo model assumes both customer and worker submit reciprocal ratings after every engagement. When customers skip the review step, Bayesian historical smoothing is required to avoid artificial score decay.

PART 7: SECTION 6 - CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In this paper, we presented Workify, a cloud-native platform designed to organize and streamline on-demand trade services in secondary municipal markets. By integrating constrained Haversine geospatial filtering, bilateral Elo reputation dynamics, multi-factor ranking, and a two-factor doorstep authentication handshake, the system delivers dispatch matching in 34.8 ms with 96.4% radial precision and zero proxy worker substitutions.

Looking ahead, we plan to extend this work along three main directions: (1) building localized Telugu speech and voice recognition interfaces [17] so that non-literate workers and elders can use the platform comfortably; (2) integrating lightweight computer vision models (such as YOLOv8) on mobile devices to assist technicians with preliminary fault inspection; and (3) exploring smart-contract micro-insurance mechanisms to offer affordable accident coverage for technicians performing hazardous electrical or structural work [25].
