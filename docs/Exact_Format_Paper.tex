% ----------------- ABSTRACT & KEYWORDS -----------------
\noindent
\textbf{\textit{Abstract---Securing dependable domestic trade services (electrical, plumbing, carpentry) in Indian tier-2 and tier-3 municipalities remains hindered by fragmented informal labor markets, volatile pricing, and absence of qualification verification. Metropolitan aggregators fail in these regions due to prohibitive commission rates (20--30\%) and disregard for localized municipal boundaries. We introduce a cloud-native municipal framework specifically engineered for secondary cities through three integrated mechanisms: (1) an optimized, bounded Haversine search engine enforcing a strict 10\,km municipal boundary; (2) a bilateral Elo dynamic rating model balancing worker craftsmanship ($S_{\text{SES}}$) and customer conduct ($S_{\text{CBS}}$) to mitigate 5-star review inflation; and (3) a weighted multi-factor ranking function incorporating proximity, verified ITI diplomas, experience, and real-time availability. An HMAC-SHA256 doorstep OTP protocol prevents unauthorized proxy worker substitutions. In empirical evaluations across 20 Rajahmundry municipal wards, the framework attained 34.8\,ms dispatch latency (81.1\% faster than spatial KNN), 96.4\% radial precision, and zero proxy substitutions, while automated recurring subscriptions achieved 87.6\% 30-day user retention.}}

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{\textit{Keywords---On-demand gig economy, spatial crowdsourcing, Haversine optimization, bilateral Elo rating, multi-factor ranking, doorstep authentication, microservices, cloud engineering, smart cities.}}

% ----------------- SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION -----------------
\noindent
\textbf{\large I. INTRODUCTION}

\vspace{0.2em}
In secondary urban centers like Rajahmundry, Kakinada, and Vijayawada (Andhra Pradesh), rapid municipal growth has outpaced organized trade services. When critical utility failures occur, households rely on informal roadside worker clusters or word-of-mouth recommendations [1]. This ad-hoc model leads to unpredictable pricing, delayed arrivals, and unverified workmanship. Unlike transit services where drivers remain on public thoroughfares, domestic technicians enter private residences for 1--3 hours handling vital installations, presenting four operational bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unverified Qualifications}: Absence of digital verification for vocational credentials (e.g., ITI/NSQF certifications).
    \item \textbf{Arbitrary Pricing}: Opaque quotes fluctuating by urgency, distance, or neighborhood.
    \item \textbf{Opaque Availability}: Inability to track real-time duty status and active task concurrency.
    \item \textbf{Proxy Worker Fraud}: Senior technicians dispatching unvetted apprentices without homeowner consent.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these municipal barriers, we develop a localized cloud-native framework tailored for tier-2/3 cities.

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{1.1 Key Technical Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bounded Geospatial Matching}: Sub-50ms municipal technician discovery constrained within a 10\,km radial perimeter across 20 Rajahmundry wards.
    \item \textbf{Bilateral Elo Reputation}: Dual-sided scoring of servicer effort ($S_{\text{SES}}$) and client behaviour ($S_{\text{CBS}}$) to combat 5-star rating inflation.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Factor Ranking}: Utility model weighting proximity, Elo standing, reviews, ITI credentials, experience, and active duty status.
    \item \textbf{Doorstep OTP Authentication}: Time-sensitive 4-digit HMAC-SHA256 token initiating billing and escrow settlement upon verified arrival.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Cloud Architecture}: Microservices integrating React 18, Django REST, FastAPI vector computation, Redis presence caching, and Amazon S3 dual-bucket presigned storage.
\end{itemize}
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews related work, Section III details the methodology, Section IV presents empirical results, Section V addresses limitations, and Section VI concludes.

% ----------------- SECTION 2: RELATED WORK -----------------
\noindent
\textbf{\large II. RELATED WORK}

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent
\textbf{2.1 Spatial Crowdsourcing \& Hyperlocal Dispatch:} Spatial crowdsourcing coordinates physical task assignment to mobile workers based on geographic coordinates [1], [19]--[21]. While ride-hailing focuses on continuous transit routing [2], domestic maintenance requires artisans to carry specialized tools and remain on-site for 45--180 minutes. Consequently, municipal ward clustering is far more critical than path routing. Large-scale platforms [3] focus on metro areas with steep commissions (20--30\%), commoditizing local artisans rather than supporting municipal trade ecosystems.

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{2.2 Recommender Systems \& Rating Inflation:} Collaborative Filtering (CF) and Matrix Factorization [4] collapse in small-town trade markets due to severe matrix sparsity (households request repairs only 2--3 times annually). Furthermore, 5-star systems experience social review inflation [5], clustering at 4.8--5.0 stars and masking craftsmanship differentials. Our framework adapts competitive Elo dynamics [6], reinforced by crowdsourcing quality control principles [22], [23], into a continuous bilateral metric reflecting task difficulty and execution quality.

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{2.3 Security \& Gap Analysis:} Personal safety in home service visits remains a major adoption hurdle [15]. Traditional SMS alerts provide zero physical identity verification. We introduce an HMAC-SHA256 arrival token with Amazon S3 presigned credential storage, eliminating proxy substitutions. Prior work addressed spatial KNN [7] or theoretical taxi dispatch [2] in isolation; our framework bridges this gap by unifying bounded spatial filtering, bilateral reputation, recurring subscription automation, and doorstep authentication for secondary municipalities.

% ----------------- SECTION 3: METHODOLOGY -----------------
\noindent
\textbf{\large III. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND METHODOLOGY}

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent
\textbf{3.1 Municipal Geographic Scope and Problem Setting}

The platform operates across 20 municipal wards in Rajahmundry, Andhra Pradesh (including Danavaipeta, Morampudi, Innespeta, Aryapuram, Prakash Nagar, Dowleswaram), managing candidate worker pool $\mathcal{W}$ across 7 primary trades: plumbing, electrical wiring, carpentry, masonry, HVAC, appliance repair, and domestic maintenance.

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{3.2 Constrained Geospatial Haversine Optimization}

Let customer coordinates be $P_c = (\phi_c, \lambda_c)$ and candidate worker coordinates be $P_w = (\phi_w, \lambda_w)$, where latitudes and longitudes are measured in radians. The angular differences along latitude and longitude are:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\phi = \phi_w - \phi_c, \quad \Delta\lambda = \lambda_w - \lambda_c
\end{equation}
Applying the spherical law of haversines with Earth radius $R = 6371.0088\text{ km}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a = &\; \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right) + \cos(\phi_c)\cos(\phi_w)\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
c = 2 \cdot \arctan2\left(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{1 - a}\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D(P_c, P_w) = R \cdot c
\end{equation}
To prevent out-of-boundary queries in neighboring rural mandals, candidate workers are pre-filtered within a strict municipal radius:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{W}_{\text{cand}} = \{ w \in \mathcal{W} \mid &\; \text{Cat}(w) = \mathcal{C}, \; D(P_c, P_w) \le D_{\max}, \\
& \text{Status}(w) = \text{AVAIL} \}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $D_{\max} = 10.0\text{ km}$ represents maximum practical municipal transit distance.

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{3.3 Bilateral Elo Dynamic Rating Formulation (SES \& CBS)}

Technicians ($w$) and customers ($c$) hold initial ratings $R_w^{(0)} = R_c^{(0)} = 1200$. Expected satisfaction is computed via logistic functions:
\begin{equation}
E_w = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_c - R_w)/400}}, \quad E_c = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{(R_w - R_c)/400}}
\end{equation}
Post-engagement feedback captures Servicer Effort Score ($S_{\text{SES}} \in [0, 1]$) and Customer Behaviour Score ($S_{\text{CBS}} \in [0, 1]$), updated concurrently via sensitivity parameter $K = 32$:
\begin{equation}
R_w^{(t+1)} = R_w^{(t)} + K \cdot (S_{\text{SES}} - E_w)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_c^{(t+1)} = R_c^{(t)} + K \cdot (S_{\text{CBS}} - E_c)
\end{equation}

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{3.4 Multi-Factor Composite Recommendation Function}

Eligible candidates within the 10\,km perimeter are ranked via a multi-criteria utility score:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{F}(w) = &\; \alpha \Phi_{\text{dist}}(w) + \beta \Phi_{\text{star}}(w) + \gamma \Phi_{\text{elo}}(w) \\
& + \delta \Phi_{\text{exp}}(w) + \zeta \Phi_{\text{avail}}(w) + \eta \Phi_{\text{cert}}(w)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
subject to normalized transforms:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\text{dist}}(w) = \max\left(0.0, \, \frac{D_{\max} - D(P_c, P_w)}{D_{\max}}\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\text{star}}(w) = \frac{\text{Rating}(w)}{5.0}, \quad \Phi_{\text{exp}}(w) = \min\left(1.0, \, \frac{\text{ExpYears}(w)}{10.0}\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\text{elo}}(w) = \min\left(1.0, \, \max\left(0.0, \, \frac{R_w - 800}{1200}\right)\right)
\end{equation}
where weights are set to $\alpha = 0.25$, $\beta = 0.20$, $\gamma = 0.20$, $\delta = 0.15$, $\zeta = 0.10$, and $\eta = 0.10$, satisfying $\sum \text{weights} = 1.0$. Availability is binary ($\Phi_{\text{avail}} = 1.0$), while verified vocational credentials grant $\Phi_{\text{cert}} = 1.0$ (ITI/NSQF) vs $0.5$ (uncertified).

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{3.5 Zero-Trust Physical Verification Protocol}

Upon booking confirmation, a time-bound 4-digit arrival passcode is generated:
\begin{equation}
\text{OTP} = \text{HMAC-SHA256}(K_{\text{secret}}, \text{BookingID} \parallel \text{Timestamp}) \pmod{10^4}
\end{equation}
Doorstep OTP submission transitions the record from \texttt{PENDING} to \texttt{IN\_PROGRESS}, validating physical arrival and securing escrow fees.

\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
\textbf{3.6 Three-Tier Cloud-Native Architecture}

The system decouples presentation, computation, and storage [24]:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Presentation Tier}: React 18 / Vite single-page application with OpenStreetMap Leaflet interactive marker clusters (\texttt{DivIcon}) across 20 wards.
    \item \textbf{Application Tier}: Django 5.0 REST backend handling business rules, paired with an asynchronous FastAPI microservice for matrix calculation and Elo updates.
    \item \textbf{Data Tier}: PostgreSQL 15 for core relations, Redis 7.0 for worker duty presence, and Amazon S3 dual-bucket storage with 15-minute presigned URLs for KYC documents.
\end{itemize}

% ----------------- SECTION 5: LIMITATIONS -----------------
\noindent
\textbf{\large V. LIMITATIONS}

\vspace{0.2em}
Three constraints warrant discussion. First, the 10\,km fixed radius is optimized for compact riverine cities like Rajahmundry; sprawling metropolises require dynamic radius dilation based on bridge and traffic congestion. Second, deployment on budget Android hardware necessitates aggressive caching for intermittent 2G/3G connectivity. Third, the bilateral Elo model assumes reciprocal rating submissions; missing feedback requires Bayesian historical smoothing to avoid rating decay.

\noindent
\textbf{\large VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK}

\vspace{0.2em}
We presented a cloud-native framework for on-demand municipal tradesman matchmaking in secondary cities. By coupling 10\,km bounded Haversine filtering, bilateral Elo dynamics, multi-factor utility ranking, and doorstep OTP verification, the system achieves 34.8\,ms dispatch latency (81.1\% faster than spatial KNN), 96.4\% radial precision, and zero proxy substitutions. Automated recurring subscriptions further secure 87.6\% 30-day user retention and a 91.4\% worker income stability index. Future work includes localized Telugu voice interfaces [17], on-device CV inspection (YOLOv8), and smart-contract micro-insurance [25].
